The problem

The old tells are gone. The ask is still there.

Cloned voices and swapped faces now pass a phone or video call. In February 2024 one finance employee in Hong Kong paid out US$25M after a video call where every colleague, including the CFO, was a deepfake. Indians lost ₹1,935 crore to “digital arrest” scams in 2024 across 1.23 lakh complaints. Gartner found 41% of CISOs had already seen a deepfake in an employee audio call.

Spotting the fake gets harder every month. But every one of these attacks has to ask for something: a wire, an OTP, an app install, a click. TrustCheck judges the content and the request together, then makes the person verify with the real sender before money or data moves.

The workflow

From a suspicious request to a stopped payment

Every input goes through one pipeline. Detectors explain themselves, context is checked against the organisation's Trust Graph, and fusion weighs the evidence by what is being asked. A HIGH verdict pauses the action until the real person answers on a channel the attacker does not control.

analysis and action flow attack path feedback loop into the blocklist

The risk engine

Context beats pixels

Detector scores are combined in log-odds from a 5% prior, then multiplied by the impact of what is being asked. Hard policy rules set a floor no detector can talk its way under, and the floor rises with the money at stake.

R = 100 × σ( b + Σ wᵢ · ln( sᵢ / (1 − sᵢ) ) ) × Impact,   b = ln(0.05 / 0.95) = −2.944

Example C is the point of the design: when the fake is perfect, the maths alone says 17, but the request breaks policy (unregistered channel, new beneficiary), so the floor makes it HIGH. Both the Python backend and the browser copy of the engine assert these examples on every run.

Features

Every other tool defends. We fight back.

Fight back

Decoy Mode

Turn the scam into evidence

On a HIGH case, scripted replies play a confused victim in English or Hinglish, so the scammer reveals bank account, IFSC, UPI id and phone. Every reply is mined.

Mule blocklist

One catch protects everyone

Identifiers from decoys and confirmed attacks are stored. Any later message or call transcript naming one scores HIGH, whoever it claims to be.

Human 2FA

A code word a clone can't know

Two words that change every 30 seconds, derived per person on the server and shown only on the real person's phone or via the Telegram bot.

Cooling-off lock

Take away the urgency

HIGH payment and credential requests are held for 30 minutes with a visible countdown. Only the real person's YES releases them early.

Real person alerted

Email and Telegram, instantly

On a HIGH case the real person gets a signed YES/NO link on their registered email and a Telegram message. NO marks a confirmed attack and drafts the CERT-In report.

Architecture

One laptop, open models, works offline

Backend

  • FastAPI with 20 routes and a WebSocket for live calls; SQLite for people, cases and mules.
  • Six models on one RTX 4050 (6 GB): pellav2, ECAPA, faster-whisper large-v3, GenD, InsightFace, UnivFD. Warm-up on start; voice note ~2.5 s, video ~4.5 s.
  • Fusion engine in Python, mirrored in the browser, both self-checked.
  • Out-of-band verification: signed HMAC links by email (SMTP) and a Telegram bot; Cloudflare tunnel so phones can reach it.

Frontend and extension

  • React 19, Tailwind 4, Framer Motion. A macOS-style console: Check, Live call, Security queue, Trust Graph.
  • Landing page: a WebGL2 raymarched landscape (terrain, water, clouds, stars, aurora) the camera flies through on scroll, adaptive to hold 60 fps.
  • Chrome MV3 Live Call Guard: tab capture to an offscreen recorder to the WebSocket.
  • Privacy: uploads deleted after analysis, only embeddings stored, code-word secrets never leave the server.

Honest limitations

Where it can be wrong

  • New voice-cloning tools appear monthly; detectors trained before them will miss some. That is why no single detector decides.
  • Phone and WhatsApp compression blur the evidence: face-swap false alarms rise to 35% on re-encoded video. We report the drop instead of hiding it.
  • A good clone passes a voiceprint check. It still can't answer YES from the real person's phone.
  • AI-written text can't be reliably detected, so we judge the manipulation, not who wrote it. New scam styles are missed until a pattern exists (42% on the newest test set).
  • The live tab-share and the Chrome extension have been tested by streaming audio, not yet on a real Meet call with a person.
